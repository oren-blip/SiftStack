## What Leaves Your Plate

    Phase 3 is not about growing headcount. It is one trade: the phones leave your plate, and a system nobody can break replaces your memory.

### Off your plate: the phones

        Callers run the dials, 150 a day each. A lead manager qualifies every answer and works the not-interested list daily. First touch to qualified lead happens without you.

### Still yours: manage and close

        You read the board every morning, coach callers on real calls, and take qualified sellers to contract. Hiring stays your job at this rung, nobody else's.

### The Replacement Ladder rule

      1. Admin

    Hire to replace yourself on the cheapest work first. Never skip a rung. Phase 3 climbs the first three rungs; leadership hires wait for Phase 4.

## The System Before the People

    Not a hiring problem. A handoff problem. A lead that lives in your memory dies the first time someone else touches your pipeline.

#### Build these before the first interview

      Statuses: every lead carries one status that tells the next person the exact next action
      Tasks: every follow-up exists as a dated task, not an intention
      Boards: one view shows every lead by stage, nothing lives in a notebook or your head
      Structured intake: every new lead lands with the same fields filled, every time
      Default cadences: 4 full attempts per record. Every number called, voicemail left, text sent, counter updated
      Not-interested list worked daily, not when someone remembers

## The Fixed Hiring Order

    Admin, prospectors, lead manager, closer. Cheapest seat first, every time. One admin plus 1-2 prospectors before any salesperson.

#### 1. Admin (Data Manager)

          $500-700/mo

          Pulls lists, skip traces, dedupes, keeps intake clean. The cheapest work leaves your plate first. One admin running Claude now covers what several used to.

#### 2. Prospectors

          $750-1,000/mo each

          Start with 1-2. Hold the 150 dials a day floor from week one. Two weeks under the floor means coach or replace, not hope.

#### 3. Lead Manager

          $1,500-3,000/mo

          Qualifies every answer, works the not-interested list daily, owns the board. About 2 callers per lead manager. Hire when caller output outruns your own follow-up.

#### 4. Closer

          $2,000/mo + 3-10% per deal

          Takes qualified sellers to contract. Commission keeps the seat hungry. Add only after the first three seats produce more appointments than you can run.

    Screen every candidate against one rubric. Onboard every seat with a written SOP. Hold the 150-dial floor from day one. The Hiring Guide in the resources below has both.

## Where Teams Are Heading

    The end state is not a longer assembly line. Fewer, better people beat more seats.

        The assembly line

#### Three seats, two handoffs

        Caller hands to lead manager. Lead manager hands to closer. Two handoffs, two places a lead can die, three salaries on one deal.

        The full-cycle seller

#### One seat, zero handoffs

        One person prospects, lead-manages, and closes. The seller who heard the first hello signs the contract. Pay one strong salary instead of three average ones.

    Same move on the admin side: one admin working Claude runs list pulls, skip tracing, intake, and reporting from a single seat. Build the split seats first, in the fixed order. Collapse them as your best people prove range.

## Graduate When the Team Runs the Market

    Three tests. Pass all three or stay at rung 3.

      1**A team runs the market.** Callers dial, the lead manager qualifies, deals close on a schedule you did not push.

      2**Lead flow is predictable.** The cadence produces every week, and you can call next month's lead count within a band.

      3**You are not the bottleneck.** A week away changes nothing but your inbox.
